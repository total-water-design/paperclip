# Total Water Academy — MBBR / Attached-Growth Teaching Source Map v1.0

## Purpose

This source map complements the existing Level 5 **Biological Treatment & Water Reuse** curriculum with practical MBBR process visualization and design-language examples from the supplied 2014 presentation on Moving Bed Biofilm Reactors (MBBR).

The presentation is useful as a teaching aid for:

- MBBR process layout;
- attached-growth versus suspended-growth concepts;
- moving carrier media;
- aerobic and anoxic staging;
- aeration/mixing;
- media-retention screens;
- downstream clarification;
- modular/package-system examples;
- design-basis reading;
- equipment/data-sheet literacy.

The presentation is **not** treated as the authoritative source for project-grade MBBR sizing. Several numerical ranges are secondary citations and the deck contains presentation-era simplifications and typographical/unit inconsistencies. Current learner-facing design criteria must be reconciled with Total Bio Design and stronger current primary/peer-reviewed references before use as design guidance.

## Academy placement

### Level 2 — Unit operations foundation

Use MBBR as an early example of coupled transport and reaction:

- substrate transport from bulk liquid to biofilm;
- oxygen transfer from bubbles to liquid and then biofilm;
- diffusion through biofilm;
- biological reaction;
- mixing and contact;
- solids generation;
- hydraulic residence time versus biomass retention.

This makes mass-transfer theory tangible before students enter full biological process design.

### Level 5 — Biological Treatment & Water Reuse

Primary integration belongs here.

Recommended teaching sequence:

1. suspended-growth versus attached-growth systems;
2. what a biofilm is;
3. why carrier media are used;
4. protected/effective surface area concept;
5. fill fraction;
6. aerobic versus anoxic MBBR zones;
7. carbon removal versus nitrification/denitrification duty;
8. oxygen and mixing requirements;
9. media-retention screens;
10. solids produced by biofilm sloughing;
11. clarification / downstream solids separation;
12. retrofit and footprint considerations;
13. process train integration with tertiary filtration, membranes and reuse.

### Level 6 — Pumps, Energy & Electrical

Use MBBR to teach blower/aeration duty as a real process-energy load and to reinforce that aeration is performing at least two functions:

- oxygen transfer;
- carrier/media mixing and movement.

Students should learn that reducing air cannot be evaluated only as an energy-saving action; it may affect dissolved oxygen, carrier movement and process performance.

### Level 7 — Instrumentation & Control

- high/low DO alarms;

If the selected biological train includes MBBR, students should defend:

## Key teaching concepts

### 1. MBBR is an attached-growth process

The important concept is not simply that plastic pieces are placed in a tank. The carriers provide protected surface where a biofilm develops while the media move through the reactor.

Students should understand the three simultaneous domains:

**bulk liquid ↔ biofilm ↔ carrier surface**

The treatment rate can be influenced by:

- bulk substrate concentration;
- dissolved oxygen;
- diffusion through the boundary layer/biofilm;
- active biofilm thickness;
- temperature;
- effective carrier surface area;
- hydrodynamics;
- loading.

### 2. HRT and biomass retention are not the same concept

One useful advantage of attached growth is that biomass is retained on carriers rather than being governed solely by the liquid hydraulic residence time.

Academy should explicitly compare:

- HRT;
- SRT in suspended activated sludge;
- attached biomass retention in MBBR.

### 3. Carrier media need engineering interpretation

Students should distinguish:

- nominal/geometric surface area;
- protected surface area;
- effective biological surface area;
- media fill fraction;
- media density/buoyancy;
- carrier geometry;
- media-retention screen requirements.

Do not teach “more quoted surface area is always better.” Hydrodynamics, protected area, biofilm accessibility and vendor/test basis matter.

### 4. Aeration is both oxygen transfer and mixing

In aerobic MBBR, air often keeps carriers moving while supplying oxygen.

This creates a useful multi-disciplinary teaching point:

**biological oxygen demand + oxygen transfer + hydrodynamics + blower energy + controls**

### 5. MBBR does not eliminate solids handling

Biofilm grows and sloughs. The downstream train still has to remove generated solids.

Depending on system architecture, this can involve:

- secondary clarification;
- lamella/tube settlers;
- flotation;
- filtration;
- membrane separation;
- sludge handling.

### 6. Staging can create different biological environments

Use two-stage or multi-stage examples to explain how reactors can be arranged for different duties, including:

- carbon removal;
- nitrification;
- anoxic denitrification;
- polishing.

Students should not assume every stage has the same DO, loading or biological purpose.

## Numerical data policy

The supplied presentation contains example design/operating values for retention time, media surface area, fill fraction, contact time and organic loading. These are useful as **historical/example ranges**, but must not become Academy design defaults without verification.

Reasons:

- source is a 2014 presentation;
- some values are attributed indirectly to older literature;
- terminology is inconsistent in places;
- at least some units/labels appear questionable or typographically corrupted;
- carrier technologies and protected-area definitions vary by manufacturer;
- modern MBBR design is load-, temperature-, objective- and media-specific.

Therefore:

> Academy may show a historical/example range to teach what a parameter means, but project design values must come from validated Total Bio Design logic and current authoritative source data.

## Interactive Academy exercises

### 1. Suspended or attached?

Show several reactor configurations and ask students to identify:

- suspended-growth activated sludge;
- fixed attached-growth;
- moving attached-growth MBBR;
- hybrid configuration.

### 2. Build the MBBR train

Student arranges:

**screening → equalization (if needed) → anoxic/aerobic biological stages → media retention → solids separation → tertiary treatment/disinfection**

The exact train depends on the design basis.

### 3. Follow a molecule

Let a BOD substrate molecule and an oxygen molecule travel through:

**bulk water → boundary layer → biofilm → biological reaction**

Connect directly to Level 2 mass-transfer concepts.

### 4. Carrier-media comparison

Provide several media data sheets with different:

- total surface area;
- protected area;
- fill fraction limits;
- density;
- geometry.

Ask the student which values actually matter and what additional information is missing.

### 5. Airflow tradeoff

Student reduces blower speed to save power.

Academy then asks them to check:

- DO;
- oxygen transfer;
- media movement;
- treatment objective;
- alarm/permissive consequences.

### 6. Nitrification challenge

Change:

- temperature;
- ammonia load;
- COD load;
- DO.

Ask students to predict qualitatively how nitrification robustness changes before Total Bio Design performs any validated calculations.

### 7. Media escape incident

Scenario: damaged/incorrect retention screen.

Students diagnose:

- carrier loss;
- reduced active surface area;
- downstream equipment impact;
- operational response.

### 8. Clarifier interface

Increase biomass sloughing/solids loading and show why the biological reactor cannot be designed independently from downstream solids separation.

### 9. Retrofit mission

Existing activated-sludge basin has limited footprint and needs increased biological capacity.

Students evaluate why attached-growth media may be considered and identify what must still be checked:

- oxygen transfer;
- mixing;
- screens;
- hydraulics;
- downstream solids separation;
- structural/equipment constraints.

### 10. Read the package-system data sheet

Use an original Academy-style equipment schedule inspired by the practical format of the presentation.

Students identify:

- design flow;
- number of trains;
- tank dimensions;
- effective volume;
- media volume;
- protected area;
- pipe connections;
- materials;
- downstream clarification;
- items marked “final design.”

Teaching goal: distinguish preliminary/vendor information from a completed engineering design.

## Guided Engineering Solution Mode — misconception library

### “MBBR is just activated sludge with plastic media.”

Why it fails: attached-growth biomass changes retention, transport and reactor behavior.

### “Carrier surface area alone sizes the reactor.”

Why it fails: loading, effective/protected area, temperature, substrate, oxygen, kinetics and hydrodynamics all matter.

### “More media is always better.”

Why it fails: fill fraction affects movement, mixing, oxygen transfer, hydraulics and practical operability.

### “MBBR needs no solids separation.”

Why it fails: biofilm sloughing and suspended solids still require downstream management.

### “Aeration is only for oxygen.”

Why it fails: in aerobic MBBR it commonly contributes to carrier movement/mixing as well.

### “A published HRT range is a design equation.”

Why it fails: HRT is an outcome of a design basis and loading/kinetic assumptions, not a universal sizing rule.

### “MBBR always eliminates sludge recycle.”

Why it fails: process configurations vary; hybrid systems and downstream solids-handling arrangements must be understood from the actual design.

## Source quality and copyright policy

The presentation may support:

- terminology;
- practical process arrangement;
- historical context;
- visual inspiration;
- questions for deeper verification.

It must not be the sole authority for:

- design loading rates;
- reactor sizing;
- media selection;
- oxygen demand;
- nitrification kinetics;
- clarifier sizing;
- performance guarantees.

Academy must create original diagrams rather than reuse presentation graphics. Numerical claims that are retained must be traced to stronger primary/peer-reviewed sources and current Total Bio Design authority where relevant.

## Engineering authority

Academy owns pedagogy and exercises only.

`app/total-bio-design` remains the specialist owner for validated biological calculations and design logic. Academy must not create a parallel MBBR sizing engine.
